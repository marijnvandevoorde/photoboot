Photoboot

Privacy policy

Draft, under review. This policy describes what the Photoboot software does today. It may still change.

Photoboot (boot.small-victories.co and the Photoboot iOS app) is run by Small Victories. Questions, or want to use your rights? Mail hello@small-victories.co.

The booth: photos stay on the device unless shared

  • The camera picture is processed in the browser or app on the booth device. Taking a photo or printing a sticker sends nothing to our server; the printer is reached over Bluetooth, straight from the device.
  • The booth device can keep a colour copy of every photo in its own browser storage, so the host can download them later. That copy never leaves the device unless the host exports it. Settings, counters and the PIN live on the device too.
  • A photo reaches our server in two cases only:
    • a guest taps Get photo to get it on their phone through a QR code;
    • the booth is set up for an event with an online gallery: then every photo taken at that booth is uploaded to the event's gallery when the guest leaves the photo screen.

Shared photos

  • We store the photo (a JPEG of the sticker layout in colour) with a random id, the time it was taken, its size and the event it belongs to. Nothing else about the guest: no name, no face recognition, no location.
  • Anyone with a photo's link can see it, so share links with care. The photo page has a Delete from the server button; that removes it for good.
  • Event photos can also be seen and downloaded by everyone who has the event's gallery link (the host, and whoever they share it with).
  • Photos are deleted automatically: 30 days after they were taken, or for an event with a paid gallery one year after (or the period agreed for that event). If an event's payment is refunded, its photos fall back to the 30 days. Deleting an event deletes all its photos.

Hosts who buy an event gallery

  • We keep the event name, the optional date and your email address, used to send you the setup link and the gallery link, and to answer you if you write to us. We don't send newsletters.
  • When a booth joins an event, it stores the event's setup on our server (settings and any header or footer images you add) and reports counters (sessions, prints, shares, the busiest hour) under a random device id.
  • The event and your email address are kept until the event is deleted. Ask us and we delete it, with its photos. Payment records are kept as long as accounting law requires.

Payments

  • On the web, Stripe handles the payment. You enter your card or other payment details with Stripe, not with us. We receive the payment's reference, amount, currency and your email address, and Stripe creates an invoice. See Stripe's privacy policy.
  • In the iOS app, Apple handles the in-app purchase. Apple doesn't tell us who you are; we receive the signed transaction (its id, the product, price, currency, App Store country and date) and a random token that ties it to your event. See Apple's privacy policy.

Who processes data for us

  • Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (Amsterdam region) runs the server, the database and the private storage for photos.
  • Cloudflare sits in front of the site, like most websites, and sees your IP address and the requests you make, to deliver and protect the site.
  • Brevo sends the email with your event links.
  • Stripe and Apple handle payments, as above.
  • Google runs Google Analytics and Google Tag Manager, only if you accept (below).

Some of these companies may process data outside the EU, under the safeguards the GDPR requires.

Cookies and analytics

  • Only the event sign-up page and the privacy and terms pages use Google Analytics, loaded through Google Tag Manager, to count visits. The booth, the settings, and the guests' photo and gallery pages never load it.
  • Nothing is tracked until you accept. A banner asks first. Until you click Accept, Google Analytics is told consent is denied and sets no cookies; Reject keeps it that way. Advertising cookies stay off, whatever you choose.
  • If you accept, Google Analytics records the pages you visit, where you came from, your approximate region and your device and browser type, using the cookies _ga and _ga_…. See how Google uses data from sites like this one.
  • Your choice is remembered in your browser (local storage). Changed your mind? Click and choose again; rejecting removes the Google Analytics cookies.
  • Other browser storage is only functional: the sign-up form is kept for the browser session while you pay, so a cancelled payment doesn't lose it.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask to see, correct or delete your data, object to its use, or take it with you. Mail hello@small-victories.co; a photo you can delete yourself from its page. You can also complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority (dataprotectionauthority.be).

Hosts and their guests

A host who runs a booth at their event decides to take and share their guests' photos. Please tell your guests that photos may be shared through a link or an online gallery, and delete a photo when someone asks.
